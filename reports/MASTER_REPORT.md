## 1. Executive Summary

**The game.** *Hosting Company Tower Defense*: you run a web-hosting company as a real-time queueing simulation with a two-directional loop over a shared Pipe. Incoming traffic is your revenue; hostile traffic is your attrition. The board is the network — graph-is-map, four topologies, literal drag-a-cable wiring — and the second board ("The Book") is the sales/contract desk that feeds and stresses it. The engine's truth is a 12-step deterministic queueing simulation where **slots, not HP**, are life: utilization ρ bends latency along ρ/(1−ρ), dependency blocking cascades, retry storms *emerge* rather than spawn. The player never sees ground truth — only an **observed layer** (latency, coverage, staleness) that degrades with un-instrumented infrastructure; knowledge itself is an economy. 34 hosting types are *data, not code* (Ruleset Cards × eras); automation is a Policy Book of `when/for/then/unless` cards — a composer, never a scripting language — executed by Ghost Hands that cost the same walk and the same hand as you. Progression is THE LONG SAVE: one Company lineage that persists across generations, collapses, and pivots. Difficulty is a sawtooth; the scorecard's stated premise (per §9.13) is that you win by *letting the right things through*, and the Hardest Lesson is raising prices and losing customers. The doc's own summary of the premise: **availability is the score, attention is the resource, truth is fog, and your own written rules can be the threat.**

**The ratified architecture (one paragraph).** Rendering is **PixiJS** as a 2.5D multi-layer compositor (five hard-bounded world layers + Vue-DOM Chrome/Annotation layer), with four spec'd 3D verbs consciously traded away (§2.1). The deterministic simulation — 12-step pipeline, Q16.16 fixed-point integer time, seeded RNG streams, ground/observed twins, and the embedded closed-enum Policy interpreter — is a **single TypeScript core, authoritative in-browser via a Web Worker** for single-player; **PHP + Workerman** own macro-tick services, persistence, transport, and the canonical ledger notary; **MySQL + Dragonfly/Redis** are system-of-record and hot-set mirror. The Company save uses the **lineage-tree schema** (one save root, generational Company nodes with inheritance manifests; ~20 write facets behind 4 read faces). **Async-Versus** is approved as a v-someday mode and the **co-op scenario design law** (every win condition must require cross-seat information) is authoring law from day 1. **One engine serves all seven scale tiers** with per-tier verb sets. **Multiplayer ships.** All modes write one Long Save through one API.

**Phase 1 is six prototypes, nothing else.** The doc's own gates (§9.13) decide whether this design is a game: **G1 bounce loop** (latency budget + aggression slider — fun in 90 s of programmer art or nothing else matters), **G2 attack-surface ledger** (building a DB visibly changes the threat deck), **G3 suspicion dial** (false positives the player agonizes over), **G4 drag-a-cable** (rack/topology dual view, tactile wiring), **G5 one quarter** (full pacing arc + Quarterly Review five-axis grade), **G6 two types on one engine** (shared-hosting + game-servers from data alone). Each maps to a concrete pre-scoped slice in §7, with a build order that puts the sim-core skeleton first because everything — including the render contract and the MP plan — consumes `observed_view` and the RNG/replay discipline.

**What this report is.** §2 records the ratified decisions and their consequences; §3 states the target architecture and the new client/server authority contract; §4 consolidates the eight specialists' requirement catalogues (IDs and §-citations preserved); Appendices A–D reproduce verbatim the four artifacts the owner asked to keep word-for-word (type-bundle schema, policy grammar, Company-lineage schema, tuning-sheet conflict table); §6 is the open-decisions register; §7 is the Phase 1 prototype plan; §8 is the merged risk register.

### 2.2 Consequences of each decision

**R-1 (Pixi) — four verbs consciously traded away:**
1. *Orbit-selected-object* — gone. Substituted by the WS-7 altitude ladder (Z1–Z4) and the authored second face.
2. *True-3D rack back-rotation* — replaced by **second authored face** (front/back as two hand-composed 2D faces; inspection flip is a state transition, not a camera move). **Two-face back-view law (2026-10-06):** the authored two-face sprite sets (front/back) are **CANONICAL** for the rack back-view verbs — the three actions only doable from the back view (WS-7 §1.5) ship on them; a flip-key swap replaces the rotate gesture. The mechanics are fully preserved; only the physical rotate gesture is replaced.
3. *Free-camera photo mode* — replaced by **2D camera + filter stack** (the doc's Museum/Aquarium/export-screenshot-as-marketing budget line survives at 2D: WS-7's Photo mode = camera transforms + era/grade filters over the compositor).
4. *3D cinematic money-shot moves* — replaced by 2D choreographed camera+dolly+layer-isolation sequences.
What is **kept from WS-7 unchanged**: five-layer compositor with hard non-blending boundaries; Hue Ledger; Two-Channel Law; 0.5 Hz heartbeat; Screen Budget ≥66% world; BudgetManager admission hooks (renderer must REFUSE over-budget draws); ObservedCell fidelity contract as the binding-layer data shape; instrument design language (exactly 5 faces); Five-Asset Skin Kit; era = 4 CSS tokens; Vue-DOM Chrome/Annotation layer (§7.16 HUD-text-never-scales); full accessibility program (Readout Mode, One-Hand, sonified SR DOM, click-to-link floor) — noting that SVG's free hit-testing/focus now must be **engineered inside Pixi + an a11y adapter** (WS-2 DR-9 closed: the fork is settled, the cost is accepted and scheduled).
The **Fold** (Iron Board ↔ The Book transition) becomes a shader crossfade + layout morph, not a camera flight.

**Cinema module (deferred, post-launch option)** — signed off 2026-10-06 alongside the R-1 re-confirmation: an **offline 3D photo-studio / cutscene renderer** (candidate libs: Three.js or Babylon) that rebuilds the facility as a simplified 3D diorama from observed-layer snapshot data plus seeded replay, and renders the Money-Shot pullback and the free-camera photo mode as a **discrete, non-gameplay mode**. Hard constraints: **no shared live camera with the Pixi renderer** (no dual-context tax during gameplay); **no gameplay-critical rendering may ever depend on true 3D**; the module consumes **presentation data only** — the same observed-layer projections Pixi consumes (§3.1, §3.3 rule 3); and because runs are seeded+replayable, it can re-stage any past moment for posters and share artifacts. Until and unless it ships, the 2D substitutions above remain the shipped behavior.

**R-2 (TS sim authoritative in-browser) — consequences:**
- One sim implementation ends WS-1's risk #1 (PHP↔second-implementation divergence) *by construction* — but **creates the replacement risk**: browser-JS vs Node parity for the port (§8, RISK-1 restated). Mitigation carried from WS-6/WS-8: integer-only core, no `Date.now`/`Math.random`/`Intl` in sim code, CI dual-runtime fixture gate.
- The **policy interpreter moves into the TS sim core** (was WS-5 "non-negotiable PHP" — VOID, §2.3-C1). Rule evaluation stays deterministic: fixed rule order, lowest-rule-id tiebreak, no RNG, rule phase at step 12.5 after the observed-layer write. Rule-book **version history and audit rows stay MySQL-side**; the sim carries only the active book (by hash) as state.
- **PHP+Workerman keep**: macro-tick services (settlement batches, email/inbox composition, market director *generation* hand-offs), persistence/notarization (§3.2), transport/relay for MP and async modes, bundle distribution, accounts/session.
- **What still needs server-side authority** (the question decision #2 forces): save trust & anti-cheat (notarized replay-verify, §3.3), canonical ledger storage (the MySQL system-of-record, §3.2), bundle/mod distribution & integrity, MP session relay (under topology (a); moot under recommended (b)), cross-save shared state (uptime streak, leaderboards, Versus deck exchange), cloud services (workshop, telemetry privacy). Player-verb *validity* never needs the server: it is established by the seed+input replay.
- **Async-Versus, Long Weekend, Analyst forward-sims, Ratchet Audit counterfactuals, postmortem replays — all require running the sim headless without a browser. That is what forces the Node port (§2.2-R-4, §3.4), and it is the technical heart of the MP-topology sub-decision (OD-5).**

**R-3 (lineage schema) — consequences:** every mode writes through the same Company API (WS-8: "all modes write one Long Save — needs the WS-6 write facets day 1, not per-mode save formats"); the 4 faces (Wall / Scrapbook / Almanac / People) are read-views only — UI may never invent a fifth face or write through a read view; ~300 h migration policy (§9.x "the Long Save is THE product") means schema versioning + one-way-door migrations are first-class features (WS-6 L25), exercised in every CI run; the mode **write-access matrix (A/B/C) is still OPEN (OD-9)** — it is the contract between every mode and the Long Save and blocks WS-8 co-op scope + WS-4 prestige tuning.

**R-4 (Async-Versus + co-op law) — consequences:** the deck/defense format ("deck schema = Wave Envelope; legality = §2.24 generator constraints as a validator; Second Answer = deck-design invariant") becomes a **day-1 serializable artifact** even though the mode ships later; the co-op scenario law ("every win condition requires information one seat holds and another lacks") becomes a **lint-able authoring rule** for all level content from day 1 (it is also what makes co-op cheap later: scenarios authored to the law never need re-writing for seats); `observed_view(seat)` becomes **first-class interface from day 1**, not a netcode bolt-on (§3.1).

**R-5 (one engine, 7 tiers) — consequences:** tier behavior is verb-set gating (WS-2's per-tier verb sets) + Ruleset Card rescale (WS-1 R-81..R-83, with the hard-ceiling exception) + the T0–T5/T6 tempo personality of contract lengths (WS-4 §6.6) — never engine forks. Engine-hook surface area is capped by WS-3's 15-item hook census and the §9.6 three-step filter; adding a hook is an engine change requiring the hook-surface review (RISK-2).

**R-6 (multiplayer yes) — consequences:** determinism is load-bearing for *netcode*, not just QA; the leak-QA property ("for all messages to seat s: no ground-truth property outside seat scope") runs forever in CI (RISK-9); co-op architecture is **delegation-bands-as-permission-system** (existing object, WS-8) — no new authority machinery; and the MP topology sub-decision below becomes the next required owner call (OD-5, PENDING).

**R-7 (Phase 1 = 6 gates) — consequences:** everything in §7; no content production, no campaign authoring, no render polish beyond programmer-art-plus until gates pass; the WS-5 headless rule slice rides alongside G1 on the same sim-core skeleton; gate failure routes are pre-decided by the doc (§9.13 G1: "if it's not fun in 90 s nothing else matters"; G6: "if two types don't diverge from data, cut types and go deeper").

**NEW SUB-DECISION (documented here, PENDING, recommendation given): MP topology under browser-authoritative sim.**
- **(a) Host-authoritative browser:** one seat's browser worker runs the session sim; Workerman relays per-seat **observed projections only** (never ground truth) between seats. Pros: zero second runtime; server code stays PHP-only; fits the "browser is authoritative" wording of R-2 literally. Cons: a player-hosted session's ground truth exists in a hostile runtime (the host's devtools can read the whole board — the seat-scope leak guard protects *other seats*, not the host from itself); browser tab-throttling/suspension directly threatens session liveness (mitigated — not solved — by WS-8's re-sim-from-seed+intent-log recovery); no headless home for the session (asynchronous resume, kick-mid-session, and operator re-host all need *someone else's* browser online); async-Versus/Long-Weekend/Analyst still need the Node port anyway, so (a) does **not** avoid the dual-runtime problem — it only avoids using the Node runtime for live sessions.
- **(b) TS sim as a portable library (RECOMMENDED):** the same package runs authoritative in-browser for single-player, on **Node for MP sessions** and for all headless requirements (Async-Versus, Long Weekend, Analyst-mode forward sims, Ratchet-Audit counterfactuals, CI determinism gates — these already force the port per R-2's consequence above); Workerman stays services/transport/notary around whichever runtime hosts the session. Pros: **one sim codebase everywhere**; determinism-as-crash-recovery becomes a *server-side* story again (host crash → re-sim on another node from seed+intent-log; no browser dependency); session state survives player disconnects natively; anti-cheat story for MP is clean (server-run sim, clients get projections only — the exact WS-8 protocol invariant, now enforced by physical seat placement); the WS-6 save-sync trust variant resolves naturally. Cons: a Node runtime to operate; strict browser↔Node parity discipline (fixed-point integers already make this tractable; CI dual-runtime fixture gate is the enforcement — RISK-1-restated); slightly widens "PHP+Workerman handles macro-tick/services" with a Node sidecar process class.
- **Effort delta:** small under R-2 (the Node port is required by approved features regardless; only its *role* differs). **Risk delta:** (b) is strictly lower for MP integrity + availability; (a) is lower for ops surface only.
- **Recommendation: (b). Status: PENDING owner sign-off (OD-5).** The rest of this report marks MP-host-specific consequences as conditional on OD-5; none of Phase 1 (§7) is blocked by it — every Phase 1 slice runs under either option, and WS-8's slice A deliberately hosts the sim on Node inside a browser app, which doubles as the proof-of-(b).

**The `observed_view(seat)` interface — first-class from day 1 (R-4).**
`observed_view(consumer) = project(ground_truth, instrumentation_state, consumer_scope)`, where `consumer ∈ {seat-1…seat-n, local-player, renderer, policy-interpreter, headless-analyst}`. Three laws, inherited verbatim from WS-1 Q2 guarantees and made *architectural* here:
1. **Everything visible is projection.** The renderer, the HUD, the Policy interpreter's conditions (WS-5 G1 recommendation: rules read the observed layer — fog degrades automation, by design), and every remote seat consume *only* their projection. Ground truth has exactly one consumer in the whole system: the 12-step pipeline itself.
2. **Projections are deterministic functions of sim state** — the aggregate/salience/fog computation lives *inside the core* (C10), so a projection is re-derivable from a replay, and per-seat scoping is a pure function, not network filtering afterthought. Leak auditing is then *testable*: property "for all projections to seat s, no ground-truth property outside scope" runs on the function itself, in CI, forever (WS-8 C fixture).
3. **The ObservedCell shape is the binding contract** (WS-7, adopted): `{value|null, coverage, freshness, resolution, certainty→stroke, confidence→blur-px, status, unknownExists}`. Degradation is a property of the binding layer, not of 200 components. Same cell shape flows worker→main→Pixi adapter, server→relay→remote seats, and save→restore.

**Fidelity-scaled widget system — the UI-as-observed-truth contract (near-verbatim TS type; the complete widget data contract):** §7.12 #11 *"low knowledge should literally make the UI vaguer… Knowledge is the only resource that changes how the game looks"* decomposes (§7.6) into exactly three axes + two modifiers:

```ts
type ObservedCell = {
  value:        number | null   // null = NO DATA (≠ 0, §8.8 graph law)
  fidelity:     { coverage: 0|1,          // per-PROPERTY not per-object (§7.6 R1)
                  freshness: Duration,     // observation latency 30–60s default

  certainty:    'inferred'|'measured'|'verified'   // → stroke weight (§8.2)
  confidence:   0..1                                   // → blur radius px (§8.2)
  status:       StatusChip                              // 12-value vocab (§8.2)
  unknownExists: boolean                                // "?" badge = known-unknown
                                                        //   visible "clearly" (§7.6 R2)
}
```
Every instrument face binds to `ObservedCell`s and **degrades mechanically, never bespoke**: coverage=0 → face replaced by "?" + outline at 100% clarity (*fog is over the state, never the existence*) + HUD Coverage % counter ("instrumented: 64% of nodes, 41% of links"); certainty → stroke weight (the doc's designated epistemics axis, survives CB palettes); confidence → uniform blur shader capped in px on the world object + soft-face widget variant; freshness stale → ghost trace detaches from live line, "no data" distinct from zero always; resolution → widget can only *offer* phenomena bands it samples — Telemetry Resolution zoom is a **binding upgrade, not a new widget**; aggregate cells carry `{count, worstState, nonNominal, trend, pinnedAny}` → same faces at any LOD. Implementation: one widget registry keyed by metric ID; hosting types bind gauge slots via the SkinKit manifest (HUD gauge slot = widget reference) — content-driven, budget-checked, and it makes the hand-wave detector mechanically testable (a "meter" with no binding/face/threshold in the manifest fails validation at authoring time). The architectural law: **degradation must be a property of the binding layer, not of 200 components** — *"any architecture where widgets themselves handle fog has already failed the §7.6 law of one structural decision."* **This `ObservedCell` contract is adopted in §3 as the wire/data shape behind `observed_view(seat)`** — with WS-7's cross-WS demand that the observed layer is **per-consumer, not per-session** (co-op asymmetric information = per-seat projections; feeds the MP topology sub-decision).

**The cleanest technical finding of WS-8 (adopted into §3):** the spec already contains the netcode architecture — as a single-player rule. §7.13 step 12: *"steps 1–11 operate on ground truth; step 12 writes the observed layer, and every UI element in the game reads that layer, not the truth"*; §7.6: *"the player may only act on observed truth."* Co-op NOC's hard rule — **"no player may ever see the whole board"** (§9.1), *enforced by protocol, not UI* — is therefore **exactly per-seat projection of the observed layer**: `observed_view(seat) = project(ground_truth, instrumentation, seat_scope)`. The single-player game needs one observer; co-op needs the same code path with a *perceiver parameter*. The client literally never receives truth it shouldn't render; the hard rule becomes a **protocol invariant, not an honor-system UI convention**. **And this rules out the classic alternative on principle, not preference:** deterministic **lockstep** (each peer simulating everything from an intent log — the AoE model) is *architecturally incompatible* with asymmetric-info co-op, because lockstep by definition makes every client compute the full ground truth; per-seat lockstep is impossible because the sim's couplings cross domains (step 8 dependency blocking means the application-seat's view of a slow DB *requires* the network-seat's ground truth). *"Server-authoritative projection is not the convenient option; it is the only option that can enforce the mode's defining law."*

## 2. Policy-Card Grammar Draft + Conflict-Detection Sketch

### 2.1 What the doc actually gives us
Four worked examples (§7.14):
```
When web-tier CPU > 85% for 2 min then scale out by 2 unless budget remaining < $200
When a drive fails then open a ticket and dispatch remote hands
  unless it is the second failure in this array, in which case page me
When load > 90% then shed class 4
When p95 > 200ms for 60s then raise inspection depth to Sample on the edge tier
```
Stated full grammar: `WHEN [condition] IF [guard] THEN [action] ELSE [escalate to]`. Stated constraints: "composed — not scripted", "vocabulary of game nouns and verbs", "never a scripting language", rules "visible, ordered, editable, auditable", each with upkeep.

### 2.2 Proposed formalization (EBNF-ish; report-only, reverse-engineered + one-clause extension)

```ebnf
policy      := rule-id , SCOPE , WHEN , [ FOR ] , THEN , [ UNLESS ] , [ ELSE ] , [ BAND ] ;
SCOPE       := "for" , selector ;            (* object | role/tag | query | class | whole estate *)
WHEN         := predicate , { "and" , predicate } ;          (* 1..3, AND-only *)
predicate    := metric , comparator , threshold ;
metric       := <name from observed-truth namespace, gated by instrumentation unlocks> ;
comparator   := ">" | "<" | "changed" | "fails" | "completes" ; (* closed enum *)
threshold    := number , unit | class-ref | event-name ;
FOR          := "for" , duration ;            (* sustained-window debounce, one temporal operator only *)
THEN         := action , { "and" , action } ; (* 1..3 actions, drawn from the manual verb set *)
action       := <closed enum of game verbs: scale-out|shed-class|page|open-ticket|dispatch-remote-hands|
                raise-inspection-depth|failover|run-runbook:NAME|drain|queue-change:… > ;
UNLESS       := guard , { "and" , guard } ;   (* guard := predicate over resources/counts/state-flags *)
ELSE         := "escalate to" , ( band-ref | role-ref | runbook-ref ) ;
BAND         := "inform" | "consult" | "execute" ;  (* per §7.14 Delegation Bands *)
```
**State budget:** rules may reference only *engine-maintained counters* surfaced as nouns — "the **second** failure in this array", "if it **recurs**" (§7.5 autopilot). No player-declared variables. Ever.

### 2.3 What it cannot express (the intended limits = the design)
1. **No loops/recursion/sequences** — multi-step procedures are Runbook Cards (R25), a separate imperative object; the policy layer is reflex-only.
2. **No user memory/state definition** — counters like "second failure" are bought nouns, not `set $x`.
3. **No arithmetic on metrics** (no ratios, differences, trends) — "what grew?" is a *diagnostic tool* (R46), deliberately never a rule predicate. Rules see thresholds, not derivatives. (Extension candidate, flagged, not assumed.)
4. **No temporal correlation** — "A happened, then within 5m B happened" is not expressible; change-correlation (R46) is again a peacetime UI, not a rule clause.
5. **No OR across WHEN** (authoring guidance): the doc's Pressure-Budget philosophy "more than 4 is noise the player cannot parse" (§1.7) generalizes — one rule = one story. (Doc never says this → Gap G2.)
6. **No rules about rules** — no "suspend rule X while incident Y"; only the manual Kill Switch (R18). → Gap G9; the real-ops need ("freeze autoscaling *during* failover") is currently unmet by grammar and unmentioned.
7. **No rate/loop breaker on the action itself** — nothing stops scale-out/scale-in oscillation except UNLESS counters; the doc *wants* oscillation as content (R7) but provides no damping clause. → Gap G7 ("cooldown" / "at most N per period" would be my minimal extension; needs owner sign-off because it edges toward scripting).
8. **Desired-state ("keep this true") is batch rung 4, a second paradigm** (§7.14) not covered by WHEN-trigger semantics — reconciler loops vs reflexes. Doc ships both in one Book without unifying. → ⚔️/Gap G11.

### 2.4 Conflict detection/display sketch
- Each rule compiles to: **read-set** (metrics in WHEN/UNLESS) and **write-set** (state deltas of THEN actions via a fixed action→effect table; the table IS the surface area, so it's small and closed).
- **Static, on save (server-side):** pairwise interference where `W(A) ∩ R(B) ≠ ∅` — classify: (a) contradictory actions on same trigger, (b) cycle A⇒B⇒A (oscillator — the scale-out/cost-cap archetype), (c) trigger-shadowing via rule *order* (earlier rule consumes the event), (d) resource contention (same selector + hand/budget pool). 40–200 rules → ≤40k pairs, microseconds; not a perf concern at any sane scale.
- **Dynamic:** dry-run (R15) as shadow-interpreter over the live tick stream — "would fire" events logged with tick number; oscillation confirmed when two rules' shadow-fires alternate ≥K times in-window. Postmortem gets a "rules in conflict at onset" lane.
- **Display:** tether crossings light amber in Policy Layer view (R13); conflict badge on both cards with "shows conflicts" per §7.14; ticker shows both rules' firings interleaved so the 3am fight is *watchable*, matching "visible in the ticker, costing money all night."

### 2.4 Save lifecycle

**Write protocol.** Modes never mutate Company directly mid-run. A run accumulates a `writes[]` batch (outcome object) that is **committed at settlement** (level end, quarter close, death, exit). This gives: atomicity (crash-safe), Ironman integrity (no mid-run save-scumming without explicit design), and museum provenance (every exhibit names its run + seed). The §9.4 stipulation that all modes "write into it" becomes: every mode ends in a write-batch whose **contents** vary by mode (§2.5).

**Versioning & migration.**
- `schema_version` triple (file / facet-schemas / sim-contract). Sim-contract version gates replay: an old run's `seed+input_log` is only re-expandable on a build whose RNG semantics hash matches — else fall back to that run's last full snapshot. Store both strategies per run.
- Migration = per-facet transform pipeline over the append-only events + projections, never destructive: **old event streams are never rewritten**; new fields get defaults; removed mechanics keep their event data with `deprecated: true` so the museum doesn't lose an exhibit ("a scar from a mechanic that no longer exists becomes a memorial entry" — consistent with §5.2's "permanent memorial").
- §9.6's one-way-door law applies here: a schema migration on a hundreds-of-hours save is the most literal one-way door in the product — ship dry-run + pre-migration full backup + downgradability window (§3, M3).

**Checkpointing.** Level-boundary commit (per write protocol) + intra-run snapshots every sim-month (business boundary ≈7 real min gives a natural cadence, §7.13) + Ironman mode = no manual saves, only these.
